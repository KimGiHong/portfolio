## Background

As meetings grew to 50–100 participants, the RN client started to wobble. Every join storm caused long black screens on page transitions, a single mute re-rendered the entire screen, and we were observing 100+ unnecessary re-renders per minute on the hot path.

What made it harder was that track events used an O(n²) structure that linearly scanned every member. 100 participants joining = 100 tracks × 100 participants compared = 10,000 comparisons, repeated on every join event.

## Approach

<RevealOnScroll client:visible>
  I started by **changing the shape of the data structures**. I added an auxiliary Map index to both the member store and the track store, turning frequently called lookups from O(n) into O(1).
</RevealOnScroll>

On the re-render side, I split whole-store subscriptions into selector-level ones (8 places). I wrapped the 5 key components — video, header, footer, chat, and participants — in `React.memo`, and where needed, spelled out prop equality with custom comparison functions.

> Instead of receiving 100 joins as 100 individual `addTrack` callbacks, I micro-batched them so they're delivered in 1–2 calls. So the work finishes within a single frame.

I fixed the page-transition black screens in two ways. I redesigned SFU `lastN` by participant-count band (≤9 people 720p, 26 or more 180p), and prefetched low-resolution tracks bidirectionally — not just for the current page but for the previous and next pages too. I cut the transition debounce from 250ms → 100ms, shortening perceived response time by 150ms.

## Results

- Hot-path comparisons on a 100-participant join: **10,000 → 100**.
- Join-storm throughput: 100 individual `addTrack` calls → **1–2 batched calls**.
- All member and track lookup functions moved from **O(n) → O(1)**.
- Page-transition black screens → eliminated after bidirectional prefetching. Debounce 250 → 100ms, **150ms faster response**.
- Re-render scope on mute / camera toggle — narrowed from the entire screen to the single affected video cell.

## What I Learned

The biggest lesson from performance work was *to leave behind measurable proof of itself*. "It's slow" is an unverifiable report, but "comparisons 10k → 100" is a baseline the next person can use to catch a regression. This is when I picked up the habit of pinning the *algorithmic complexity* as a comment on every hot path.

One more thing: in an RN environment, *one level of data structure → one level of memoization → one level of resource policy* need to be bundled together with equal weight to have an effect. With only the Map index, the re-renders survive; with only `React.memo`, the hot path survives; with only `lastN`, the client-side cost survives. This work confirmed that unless all three layers move together, none of them is enough on its own.
